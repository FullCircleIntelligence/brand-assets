# Course A — Fair Housing Foundations (Vermont)

**Audience:** All staff
**Duration:** ~90 minutes core, plus role tracks
**Cadence:** Annual; within 30 days for new hires
**Prerequisite for:** Course B

**Role tags used throughout:**
`ALL` all staff · `LS` leasing · `RPM` residential property manager ·
`AM` association/HOA manager · `MT` maintenance · `AC` accounting ·
`LEAD` supervisors, executives, owners

---

## Module 1 — Why This Exists (10 min) · `ALL`

**Objective:** Learners can state, in their own words, what fair housing law
protects against and why the company treats it as a top-tier risk.

1.1 A short, honest history — the Civil Rights Act of 1866, redlining, racial
covenants, the 1968 Fair Housing Act, the 1988 Amendments adding disability and
familial status, and Vermont's own expansion of the list.
1.2 What discrimination looks like in 2026: rarely a slur, usually an
inconsistent process, an unexamined rule, or a well-meant assumption.
1.3 Who can bring a claim, and the three doors: Vermont Human Rights Commission,
HUD/FHAP, and private lawsuit in state or federal court. Plus the Vermont
Attorney General's Civil Rights Unit.
1.4 Consequences that are real: actual and punitive damages, civil penalties,
attorney's fees, injunctive relief, consent decrees with monitoring, insurance
consequences, reputational damage, and personal liability for the individual
employee — not just the company.
1.5 Testers. Paired testing is lawful, routine in Vermont, and specifically used
by the CVOEO Fair Housing Project. You will not know it is a test. The only
defense is doing it the same way every time.

**Assessment idea:** Two near-identical inquiry emails; learner identifies what
made the responses inconsistent.

## Module 2 — Who Is Protected (15 min) · `ALL`

**Objective:** Learners can recite the Vermont list without prompting and can
explain that Vermont, not federal law, is the operating standard here.

2.1 **The seven federal protected classes** — race, color, religion, national
origin, sex (including sexual orientation and gender identity as interpreted by
courts), familial status, disability.
2.2 **The additional Vermont classes** — age, marital status, sexual orientation,
gender identity, receipt of public assistance, victim/survivor of domestic
violence, sexual assault, or stalking, and (since July 1, 2025, Act 69)
citizenship and immigration status.
2.3 **Teach the combined Vermont list as *the* list.** Do not teach the federal
seven and then append. Staff must not have to do a two-step to answer a question.
2.4 Each class, unpacked with a housing example and a common trap:
  - *Race and color* — including the § 1982 overlay, which has no exemptions
  - *National origin* — accent, name, language, country of origin
  - *Religion* — including holiday decorations, common-room use, and dietary needs
  - *Sex* — including sexual harassment and quid pro quo (see Module 6)
  - *Sexual orientation / gender identity* — names and pronouns in records, ID
    mismatch on applications, roommate matching
  - *Familial status* — households with anyone under 18, pregnancy, and those in
    the process of obtaining custody. The single most common trap in association
    work.
  - *Age* — a Vermont class with no federal analogue; interacts with HOPA 55+/62+
  - *Marital status* — a Vermont class; co-applicant and guarantor rules
  - *Disability* — headline only here; Course B is the depth
  - *Receipt of public assistance* — Vermont's functional source-of-income
    protection; vouchers, SSI/SSDI, GA, fuel assistance, RUAD/emergency housing
  - *Survivor status* — abuse, sexual assault, stalking; nuisance/lease-violation
    interactions
  - *Citizenship and immigration status* — Act 69, effective July 1, 2025
2.5 **Act 69 operational rules for applications** `LS` `RPM` `AC`
  - You may not require a Social Security number as a condition of applying
  - You must accept alternative government-issued identification
  - You may not deny an application solely for failure to provide an SSN
  - You may not treat applicants differently based on citizenship or immigration
    status
  - Screening must be adapted so that a no-SSN applicant is evaluated on
    equivalent alternative evidence, not simply failed by the vendor's default
  - Scope note: this is a Vermont fair housing obligation, distinct from federal
    I-9 employment verification, which does not apply to residents
2.6 What is *not* a protected class — and why "not protected" does not mean "safe"
(students, occupation, smokers, pet owners, credit score): a facially neutral
rule can still be unlawful if it functions as a proxy or is applied unevenly.
2.7 Local overlays: confirm the ordinances for each municipality you operate in
(Burlington in particular) rather than assuming the state list is the ceiling.

**Assessment idea:** Sorting exercise — 20 characteristics into
protected-in-Vermont / protected-federally-only / not protected.

## Module 3 — Where the Law Applies (10 min) · `ALL`

**Objective:** Learners stop assuming exemptions apply to Full Circle's work.

3.1 Covered transactions: rental, sale, terms and conditions, services and
facilities, financing, brokerage, advertising, and post-move-in treatment.
3.2 **It applies after move-in.** More than half of fair housing exposure in a
property management company is in tenancy administration and rule enforcement,
not in leasing.
3.3 Associations and HOAs are covered `AM`. A condo or homeowners association is
not a landlord, but it is a housing provider: its rules, its architectural review,
its common-area management, its collections, and its board's statements are all
within reach of the Act.
3.4 Exemptions under 9 V.S.A. § 4504 — and why they almost never help you:
  - Owner-occupied building of three or fewer units where the owner or an
    immediate family member lives in one unit — **the advertising prohibition
    still applies**
  - Religious organizations, non-commercial, with the preference stated in
    written policies
  - Note: exemptions do not apply where a real estate professional is involved,
    and 42 U.S.C. § 1982 (race) has no exemption at all
  - **Company policy overlay:** state plainly that Full Circle does not rely on
    exemptions, so staff never have to evaluate one on the phone
3.5 Age-restricted communities `AM` — HOPA 55+ and 62+ requirements, the
verification burden, the 80% rule, and the consequence of losing the exemption.

## Module 4 — Marketing, Advertising, and First Contact (15 min) · `LS` `RPM` `AM` `LEAD`

**Objective:** Learners can write and screen a listing, and can handle an inquiry
identically every time.

4.1 Discriminatory statements and preferences in ads — the "ordinary reader"
standard. Word-level review: "perfect for," "ideal for," "mature," "quiet
building," "no kids," "Christian home," "English-speaking," "walking distance,"
"executive," "must be employed."
4.2 Describing the *property*, not the *desired resident*. The single best
editorial rule you can give staff.
4.3 Photographs, models, and imagery — human diversity in marketing materials.
4.4 The Equal Housing Opportunity logo and statement — where it must appear.
4.5 **Digital advertising and audience targeting** — geographic radius exclusions,
lookalike audiences, age and parental-status targeting, and platform ad tools.
Note that HUD's April 2024 digital advertising guidance was withdrawn on
September 17, 2025; the *statute* did not change, and platform settings that
exclude protected groups remain a live liability under 42 U.S.C. § 3604(c).
4.6 Where you advertise is itself a fair housing choice — advertising only by
word of mouth, only in one publication, or only to one employer's staff can
have an exclusionary effect.
4.7 **Inquiry handling scripts** `LS` — the same information, the same
availability, the same offer of a showing, to every caller. No screening
questions before you give out information. No "how many children?" No "where are
you from?" No "will your husband be signing?"
4.8 Guest cards, CRM notes, and internal shorthand — write nothing you would not
read aloud in a deposition. Never record a protected characteristic as a note.
4.9 Waitlists, availability logs, and hold policies — consistent, dated, written.

**Assessment idea:** Red-line a real (anonymized) Full Circle listing.

## Module 5 — Applications, Screening, and Selection (15 min) · `LS` `RPM` `AC` `LEAD`

**Objective:** Learners apply one written standard to every applicant and can
explain any deviation in writing.

5.1 Written, published, objective screening criteria — the foundation of every
defense.
5.2 Applying criteria in the same order, with the same documents, for everyone.
First-come-first-served with a dated log.
5.3 **Income standards and public assistance** `AC`
  - Refusing a Housing Choice Voucher in Vermont implicates the
    receipt-of-public-assistance protection
  - The rent-multiplier trap: applying "3x the rent" to *contract rent* rather
    than to the *resident's portion* is the most common way voucher holders are
    screened out; teach the correct calculation explicitly
  - Requiring a guarantor, a larger deposit, or extra references only of
    assistance recipients
  - Counting SSI, SSDI, TANF/Reach Up, child support, and fuel assistance as
    income
5.4 **Credit screening** — thin-file and no-credit applicants, medical debt,
alternative evidence of rent payment history.
5.5 **Criminal record screening** — the highest-risk screening topic.
  - No blanket bans; arrests without conviction are not evidence of conduct
  - Individualized assessment: nature and severity of the offense, time elapsed,
    evidence of rehabilitation, relationship to tenancy risk
  - Note the federal shift: HUD withdrew its 2022 implementation memo on criminal
    records guidance on September 17, 2025, and has said it will de-prioritize
    disparate impact theories. The statute and Vermont law are unchanged, private
    plaintiffs are unaffected, and a blanket ban remains a poor risk. Teach the
    individualized assessment as company policy regardless of federal posture.
5.6 **Tenant screening vendors and algorithmic scoring** — you own the outcome of
your vendor's model. Understand what your provider scores, whether it produces
accept/deny recommendations, and whether it treats voucher holders differently.
Reference the SafeRent settlement (final approval November 2024, $2.28M) as the
cautionary case. Require an override path for individualized assessment.
5.7 **Adverse action** — FCRA notice obligations, the applicant's right to the
report and to dispute, and consistent documentation of the reason.
5.8 **Occupancy standards** — the two-persons-per-bedroom starting point from the
1998 Keating Memorandum (63 FR 70982) is a *starting point*, not a safe harbor;
unit size, bedroom size, configuration, and the local health code all factor in.
Overly restrictive occupancy limits are familial status discrimination.
Cross-check against the Vermont Rental Housing Health Code.
5.9 Application fees and deposits — uniform, disclosed, and lawful under Vermont
security deposit rules (9 V.S.A. § 4461).
5.10 Recording the decision — the file must show the criterion applied and the
fact that supported it.

**Assessment idea:** Three application files with identical qualifications and
different protected characteristics; learner selects and justifies.

## Module 6 — Steering, Harassment, and Retaliation (15 min) · `ALL`

**Objective:** Learners recognize the three post-inquiry conduct risks that most
often produce personal liability.

6.1 **Steering** — directing people toward or away from buildings, floors, wings,
or neighborhoods based on a protected characteristic. Includes "helpful" steering:
"there are more families in Building C," "you'd be more comfortable near the other
seniors," "that neighborhood has a great church." Also includes what you show and
what you fail to mention.
6.2 **Harassment — 24 CFR 100.7** `ALL`
  - Quid pro quo: conditioning any housing benefit on a sexual or other favor.
    Maintenance and leasing staff are the two highest-risk roles because of
    unsupervised access and discretion over service.
  - Hostile environment: severe or pervasive conduct that interferes with the use
    and enjoyment of a dwelling.
  - **Resident-on-resident harassment** — the provider can be liable for failing
    to take prompt corrective action against a resident harassing another resident
    on a protected basis, where the provider knew and had the power to act. This
    is a critical and widely missed point for both `RPM` and `AM`.
  - Direct and vicarious liability — the company is liable for its agents'
    conduct; supervisors and executives cannot rely on not knowing. `LEAD`
6.3 **Retaliation — 42 U.S.C. § 3617 and 9 V.S.A. § 4465** — coercing,
intimidating, threatening, or interfering with anyone who exercised a fair
housing right or helped someone else do so. Includes retaliation against an
employee who reported a concern, and against a resident who requested an
accommodation. Watch for the timing of notices, inspections, fee enforcement, and
non-renewals after a complaint.
6.4 Social media, group chats, and association Facebook groups `AM` — statements
by board members and by staff are evidence.
6.5 The complaint intake duty — every employee's obligation to escalate a
harassment or discrimination report the same day, to a named role.

## Module 7 — Living There: Tenancy, Rules, and Association Governance (15 min)

### 7A — Rental operations `RPM` `LS` `MT`
7A.1 Consistent enforcement of lease terms, notices, and fees — the enforcement
log as your defense artifact.
7A.2 Differential service: response times, quality of repairs, amenity access,
parking assignments, storage.
7A.3 Transfers, renewals, non-renewals, and rent increases applied consistently.
7A.4 Rules about children — curfews, pool and playground rules, supervision
requirements, "no ball playing," restricting children to certain areas. Almost
all age-based restrictions on children are familial status violations. Frame
rules around *behavior*, never around *age*.
7A.5 Guests, occupancy changes, and adding household members.
7A.6 Language access — communicating with residents with limited English
proficiency. HUD's 2007 LEP guidance was withdrawn in September 2025, but
national origin discrimination is unchanged and Vermont has significant New
American and refugee communities. Company policy should set an interpretation
standard regardless.
7A.7 Survivor protections — abuse, sexual assault, and stalking as a Vermont
protected class; lock changes, nuisance enforcement, and lease bifurcation.
Add VAWA obligations if the property is federally assisted.
7A.8 Evictions and terminations — consistency, documentation, retaliation timing,
and the interaction with a pending accommodation request.
7A.9 Vermont lead law and families with children `MT` `RPM` — Essential
Maintenance Practices for pre-1978 rentals under 18 V.S.A. § 1759, the annual
EMP compliance certificate, and lead-safe work practices. Failure here lands
disproportionately on households with young children.

### 7B — Association and HOA operations `AM`
7B.1 The association as a housing provider — the Act reaches rules, enforcement,
common areas, architectural review, and collections.
7B.2 **Selective enforcement** — the dominant association claim. Enforce the same
covenant against the same conduct every time, and keep the log.
7B.3 Rules review for disparate effect: pool and common-area rules aimed at
children, guest restrictions, English-only signage, satellite dish and religious
display rules, parking and vehicle rules.
7B.4 Architectural review committees — ramps, lifts, widened doors, handrails,
visual doorbells, and other requests are *reasonable modification* requests, not
ordinary ARC applications, and must be routed to the Course B process. `AM`
7B.5 Assistance animals versus pet covenants and weight/breed limits — the
covenant does not override the accommodation obligation. Route, do not deny.
7B.6 Board conduct: meeting minutes, email threads, comments at annual meetings,
private social media groups.
7B.7 Accessible meetings and effective communication — location, notice format,
remote participation.
7B.8 Common areas open to the public (rental or sales offices, some clubhouses
and pools) may trigger ADA Title III in addition to the Fair Housing Act.
7B.9 Leasing caps, rental restrictions, and resale approval — applied uniformly,
and examined for exclusionary effect.
7B.10 Governance overlay: 27A V.S.A. (Uniform Common Interest Ownership Act) for
communities created on or after January 1, 1999, and 27 V.S.A. ch. 15
(Condominium Ownership Act) for older condominiums. Fair housing law overrides
inconsistent covenants; a covenant is not a defense.

### 7C — Maintenance in the field `MT`
7C.1 Same response standard for every unit, every building, every resident.
7C.2 Entry, notice, and privacy — Vermont entry rules under 9 V.S.A. ch. 137.
7C.3 Conduct in occupied units: what you say, photographs, social media, comments
about a resident's household, religion, family, or disability. Quid pro quo risk.
7C.4 **Never remove, disable, or relocate an accessibility feature** — grab bars,
ramps, lever handles, visual alarms, accessible parking spaces, curb cuts, or
accessible-route clearances — without written direction from a manager.
7C.5 Recognizing a request in the wild: "could you put a bar in the shower," "the
handle is hard for me," "I can't hear the buzzer," "my doctor says I need." These
are accommodation and modification requests. Route them the same day. Do not
promise, do not decide, do not ask about the disability.
7C.6 Assistance animals encountered on the job — do not question the resident,
do not report the animal as a lease violation, do not refuse to enter.
7C.7 Service tickets and notes written in neutral, factual language.

### 7D — Accounting and administration `AC`
7D.1 Uniform application of late fees, payment plans, NSF charges, and
collections. Inconsistent leniency is a fair housing risk, not just an AR risk.
7D.2 **No pet fee, pet rent, or extra deposit for an assistance animal** — this
is explicit in Vermont law for disability-related modifications and deposits, and
it is the most common accounting-side violation.
7D.3 Rent due dates and benefit deposit dates — a request to shift the due date or
waive a recurring late fee because SSI/SSDI arrives on the third is a *reasonable
accommodation request*. Route it; do not decline it as a policy matter.
7D.4 Voucher and HAP administration — payment portions, the multiplier trap
(Module 5.3), and never requiring a voucher holder to meet a standard others do
not.
7D.5 Ledger coding and account notes — never code, tag, or annotate an account by
protected characteristic, disability, animal type, or immigration status.
7D.6 **Confidentiality of disability information** — if medical or verification
documentation reaches accounting, it does not belong there. Do not open, do not
file it in the ledger, return it to the designated custodian. Disability
information is kept separately and disclosed only on a need-to-know basis.
7D.7 Third-party payers, guarantors, and co-signers applied uniformly.
7D.8 Record retention — how long, where, and who can see it.

## Module 8 — Recognize and Route (MANDATORY) (10 min) · `ALL`

**Objective:** No employee ever answers an accommodation or modification request
on their own authority, and no request is ever lost.

8.1 A request can come from anyone — resident, applicant, household member,
family member, advocate, caseworker, or attorney.
8.2 A request can arrive **in any form** — spoken, texted, in a maintenance
ticket, in a comment at an association meeting, in a note taped to a door. It
does **not** have to use the words "reasonable accommodation," be in writing, be
on your form, or mention a disability by name.
8.3 What every employee does: acknowledge, thank, do not ask about the diagnosis,
do not say yes, do not say no, do not say "we don't allow that," and forward to
[NAMED ROLE] within [X business hours] using [CHANNEL].
8.4 What every employee must never say. A short, memorized list of prohibited
responses: "we have a no-pets policy," "you'd need a doctor's note first," "we
don't do that here," "that's not in the covenants," "you'd have to pay for that,"
"what's your condition?"
8.5 Delay is denial. An unreasonably slow response is treated as a refusal.
8.6 If your role processes requests, you must complete **Course B** before you
touch one.

## Module 9 — Company Systems and Getting Help (5 min) · `ALL`

9.1 Full Circle's fair housing policy, non-retaliation policy, and the named
fair housing officer.
9.2 Where the forms live: accommodation request intake, decision letter templates,
verification request template, enforcement log, screening criteria.
9.3 Documentation standard: contemporaneous, factual, neutral, dated.
9.4 Escalation paths for a complaint, a threat of a complaint, an HRC or HUD
notice, or a demand letter — including "do not respond directly; notify [role]
immediately."
9.5 External resources: Vermont Human Rights Commission, CVOEO Fair Housing
Project, Vermont Attorney General Civil Rights Unit, HUD FHEO.
9.6 The one-year Vermont HRC filing deadline, and what that means for record
retention.

## Assessment and completion requirements

- 20-question scenario-based assessment; 80% to pass; two retakes.
- At least 8 of 20 questions must be role-specific (branched by cohort).
- At least 4 questions must be Vermont-specific and unanswerable from federal law
  alone.
- Signed acknowledgment of the fair housing policy and the Module 8 routing rule.
- Completion recorded with course version number and date.

## Design requirements for the course itself

The course must model what it teaches: WCAG 2.1 AA, captions and transcripts on
all video, keyboard navigation, no color-only information, screen-reader-tested
assessments, and an accessible-format request path stated on the first screen.
