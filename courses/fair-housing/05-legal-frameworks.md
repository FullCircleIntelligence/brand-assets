# Legal Frameworks the Course Must Be Built On

Researched September 2026. Every dated statement below should be re-verified at
each annual re-issue. This is a curriculum reference, not legal advice.

---

## 1. Vermont law — the operating baseline

Teach Vermont first. Vermont's protected class list is broader than the federal
list, and Vermont enforcement is unaffected by federal policy shifts.

### Vermont Fair Housing and Public Accommodations Act (VFHPA)
**9 V.S.A. § 4500 et seq.** (Title 9, Chapter 139)

| Section | Subject |
|---|---|
| § 4501 | Definitions |
| § 4502 | Public accommodations |
| § 4503 | Unfair housing practices — the core operative section |
| § 4504 | Rental of housing; exemptions |
| § 4506 | Civil action; damages and remedies |

**Protected classes in housing (as enforced by the Vermont Human Rights
Commission):** race, color, sex, religion (religious creed), national origin,
disability, sexual orientation, gender identity, marital status, age, presence of
minor children (familial status), receipt of public assistance, status as a
victim of abuse / sexual assault / stalking, and **citizenship and immigration
status**.

**Key subsections to teach:**
- **§ 4503(a)(1)** — the core prohibition and the protected class list
- **§ 4503(a)(3)** — discriminatory notices, statements, and advertisements
  (this one survives even the owner-occupied exemption)
- **§ 4503(a)(9)** — reliance on aids including attendants, specially trained
  animals, wheelchairs, and similar devices; **reasonable modifications** at the
  requester's expense; the owner may require restoration to prior condition,
  reasonable wear excepted; **no additional security deposit may be demanded**
- **§ 4503(a)(10)** — **reasonable accommodations** in rules, policies, practices,
  or services when necessary to afford a person with a disability equal
  opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling unit
- **Assistance animals** — Vermont prohibits refusing to permit an assistance
  animal at a dwelling *whether or not the animal has received special training*,
  subject to individualized defenses (direct threat to health or safety,
  substantial physical damage, substantial interference with others' reasonable
  enjoyment, or not a reasonable accommodation). Verify the current subsection
  designation against the live statute when building the course.

> **Interpretive note for the course author:** Vermont's prohibitions have been
> construed in line with the federal Fair Housing Act, its implementing
> regulations, and HUD interpretive guidance. Because HUD withdrew several
> guidance documents in September 2025 (see §3 below), the course should teach
> Vermont's *statutory text* and the VHRC's own materials as primary, and treat
> withdrawn HUD guidance as persuasive best practice rather than as binding
> authority. Do not teach the withdrawn documents as current federal law, and do
> not teach their withdrawal as a change in Vermont obligations.

### Act 69 (2025) — Housing Access for Immigrant Families
Effective **July 1, 2025**. Added citizenship and immigration status as a
protected class across the subsections of 9 V.S.A. § 4503(a). Operationally:
landlords may not require a Social Security number as a condition of a rental
application, must accept government-issued identification as an alternative, and
may not deny an application solely for failure to provide an SSN.

### Vermont Human Rights Commission — enforcement mechanics
- Enforces the VFHPA; also enforces federal fair housing law on HUD's behalf
  under a Fair Housing Assistance Program contract
- **Filing deadline: within one year of the last alleged discriminatory act**
- Complaints must be written and sworn before a notary
- Respondent has **14 days** to respond
- Staff investigation target: **6 months**; full administrative process: **12
  months**
- Parties receive the investigative report and may respond within 14 days
- Commissioners issue a determination; up to a six-month negotiation period
  follows
- Where conciliation fails on a complaint also filed with HUD, litigation follows

### Federal guidance still standing
- **HUD/DOJ Joint Statement, "Reasonable Accommodations Under the Fair Housing
  Act"** (May 17, 2004) — not on the withdrawal list; still the clearest
  articulation of the interactive process and verification limits
- **HUD/DOJ Joint Statement, "Reasonable Modifications Under the Fair Housing
  Act"** (March 5, 2008) — not on the withdrawal list
- **HUD/DOJ Joint Statement on State and Local Land Use Laws and Practices**
- **Fair Housing Act Design Manual** (HUD) — design and construction
- **Keating Memorandum on occupancy standards** (1998), 63 FR 70982 — the
  two-persons-per-bedroom starting point; not a safe harbor

## 3. What changed federally in 2025–2026 — teach this explicitly

The course must state the current federal posture accurately, and must be
unambiguous that **Vermont obligations are unchanged**.

### HUD FHEO withdrawal of guidance, effective September 17, 2025
Eight documents withdrawn:

1. Guidance on Application of the Fair Housing Act to the Advertising of Housing,
   Credit, and Other Real Estate-Related Transactions through Digital Platforms
   (April 29, 2024)
2. **FHEO Notice 2020-01** — Assessing a Person's Request to Have an Animal as a
   Reasonable Accommodation Under the Fair Housing Act (January 28, 2020)
3. FHEO Memorandum on Source of Income Testing Activities under the Fair Housing
   Assistance Program (February 12, 2024)
4. **FHEO Notice 2013-01** — Service Animals and Assistance Animals for People
   with Disabilities in Housing and HUD-funded Programs (April 25, 2013)
5. FHEO Statement on the Fair Housing Act and Special Purpose Credit Programs
   (December 7, 2021)
6. Final Guidance to Federal Financial Assistance Recipients Regarding Title VI
   Prohibition Against National Origin Discrimination Affecting Limited English
   Proficient Persons (January 22, 2007)
7. Implementation of Executive Order 13988 on the Enforcement of the Fair Housing
   Act (February 11, 2021)
8. Implementation of OGC Guidance on Application of Fair Housing Act Standards to
   the Use of Criminal Records (June 10, 2022)

HUD subsequently published a Federal Register notification of withdrawal of FHEO
guidance documents (April 6, 2026) — **verify whether that notice expands the
list before finalizing the course.**

### Enforcement posture
HUD has said it will prioritize cases with strong evidence of **intentional**
discrimination and de-prioritize disparate impact theories and complaints
originating from fair housing organizations and testers.

### Disparate impact rule
HUD published a proposed rule on **January 14, 2026** to rescind 24 C.F.R.
§ 100.500, with comments due February 13, 2026, and a related August 10, 2026
Federal Register action addressing Title VI disparate impact provisions. HUD's
stated position is that the scope of disparate impact liability should be
developed by courts rather than by regulation — it is **not** asserting that
disparate impact liability is unavailable under the statute.

### What did not change
- The Fair Housing Act statute itself
- 24 C.F.R. Part 100 apart from what is formally amended
- Private rights of action in federal and state court
- **Vermont law and Vermont Human Rights Commission enforcement**
- Any municipal ordinance

Also note *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo* (2024): courts no longer defer to
an agency's interpretation, which cuts in both directions — HUD's *withdrawal* of
an interpretation is no more binding on a court than the interpretation was.

**Course drafting rule:** never say "HUD says you must…" for any withdrawn
document. Say "Vermont law requires…" or "as a matter of Full Circle policy…"
and note the federal history separately.

## 4. Case law and enforcement patterns worth teaching

- **SafeRent Solutions settlement** — $2.28M, final approval November 20, 2024;
  algorithmic tenant screening alleged to disproportionately score down Black and
  Hispanic applicants and voucher holders; the settlement bars displaying scores
  and accept/deny recommendations for voucher applicants. Use it to teach vendor
  due diligence and the individualized-review override.
- DOJ and HUD statements of interest in algorithmic screening litigation (2023).
- Design and construction consent decrees — the recurring pattern of
  post-1991 buildings that were never built to the seven requirements.
- Sexual harassment enforcement against landlords and maintenance staff — a
  sustained DOJ priority area and the clearest source of personal liability.
