# Recommendation: Two Courses, One Required Sequence

## The question

Should reasonable accommodations (RA) live inside the fair housing course, or be
its own course?

## The recommendation

**Two courses, sequenced, with Course A as a hard prerequisite for Course B.**

```
                 ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
                 │  COURSE A — Fair Housing Foundations     │
                 │  ~90 min · ALL STAFF · annual            │
                 │  incl. Module 8: "Recognize and Route"   │
                 └───────────────────┬─────────────────────┘
                                     │ prerequisite
                 ┌───────────────────▼─────────────────────┐
                 │  COURSE B — Disability, Reasonable       │
                 │  Accommodations & Modifications          │
                 │  ~120 min · all client-facing +          │
                 │  maintenance + AR/collections · annual   │
                 └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Why separate — six reasons

**1. They teach different *kinds* of competence.**
Course A is mostly *recognition and restraint*: know the protected classes, know
what not to say, apply criteria consistently, don't steer. It is knowledge and
judgment. Course B is a *procedure with artifacts and clocks*: intake, the
interactive process, what verification you may and may not request, the
undue-burden and fundamental-alteration analysis, who pays, the written decision,
the file. Procedural training needs job aids, forms, decision trees, and
practice reps. Bolting a procedure onto an awareness course produces a module
people watch rather than learn.

**2. Disability is where the claims actually are.**
Disability is consistently the largest single basis of fair housing complaints
nationally, and RA/RM denials are the most common fact pattern within it. Your
training minutes should be weighted to your risk, and a combined course
inevitably compresses RA into the last twenty minutes when attention is lowest.

**3. Different audiences need different depth of the *same* topic.**
Your accounting team does not adjudicate accommodation requests, but they do sit
on top of three classic RA fact patterns: refusing to waive a late fee tied to a
benefits deposit date, charging a pet fee or extra deposit for an assistance
animal, and applying an income multiplier to full contract rent instead of the
resident's portion for a voucher holder. They need Course A plus a targeted slice
of Course B — not the full interactive-process curriculum. A single monolithic
course forces you to either bore them or under-train the managers.

**4. The two bodies of law update on different clocks.**
Course A's core — the protected class list, advertising, steering, consistent
criteria — is stable. Course B's federal underpinnings are actively moving: HUD
withdrew FHEO Notice 2020-01 and FHEO Notice 2013-01 (both assistance-animal
guidance) on September 17, 2025, and proposed rescinding the disparate impact
rule on January 14, 2026. You will re-version Course B more than once a year.
Separate courses mean a smaller, cheaper, faster re-issue and a cleaner training
record of *which* version each employee took.

**5. It produces a better defense record.**
If you are defending an accommodation denial before the Vermont Human Rights
Commission, "the decision-maker completed a dedicated 2-hour reasonable
accommodation course on [date], covering verification limits and the interactive
process, scoring 92%" is a materially stronger record than "it was module 7 of
our annual fair housing training." Separate completion records, separate
assessments, separate certificates.

**6. Scheduling reality.**
A single 3.5-hour combined course gets deferred, split across sessions, or
half-completed. Two right-sized courses get finished. Completion rate is a
compliance metric, not just an ops metric.

## Why *not* fully separate — and how to mitigate

The real risk of splitting is that staff come to think of disability as a
specialty topic rather than as fair housing. Mitigate it deliberately:

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| "RA is someone else's job" | Course A ends with **Module 8: Recognize and Route** — a mandatory 15-minute segment stating that *any* employee can receive a request, that a request need not use the words "reasonable accommodation," that no one outside the designated decision-makers may say yes or no, and exactly whom to forward it to and within what timeframe. |
| Disability treated as separate from fair housing | Both courses open with the same protected-class list and the same "Vermont vs. federal" frame. Course B is branded as *Fair Housing II*, not as a disability course. |
| Two glossaries drift apart | One shared glossary (`06-glossary.md`) governs both. |
| Fragmented compliance tracking | Both courses roll up to one annual "Fair Housing Certified" status in the LMS. Neither alone satisfies it for staff whose role requires both. |
| Staff take B without A | Enforce the prerequisite in the LMS, not by policy memo. |

## Cadence and assignment

| Cohort | Course A | Course B | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leasing | Annual | Annual, full | Highest tester exposure; add a mid-year 20-min refresher on advertising + Act 69 application rules |
| Residential property managers | Annual | Annual, full | RA/RM decision-makers |
| Association / HOA managers | Annual | Annual, full + association add-on | Architectural review and rules enforcement modules are essential |
| Maintenance | Annual | Annual, maintenance track (~60 min) | Emphasis: recognize/route, don't decide, don't remove accessibility features, conduct in units |
| Accounting / AR | Annual | Annual, finance track (~45 min) | Fees, deposits, income calculation, benefit-date accommodations, confidentiality of medical documentation |
| Executives / owners | Annual | Annual, full | Vicarious liability under 24 CFR 100.7 runs to them |
| New hires | Within 30 days of hire | Within 60 days of hire | Before independent resident contact |
| Contractors / vendors with unit access | Course A short form (~30 min) | Awareness only | Contract clause + acknowledgment |

## Two things to explicitly scope *out*

State these boundaries in the course itself so staff don't conflate them:

1. **Employment accommodations.** ADA Title I / 21 V.S.A. § 495 accommodations
   for *your own employees* are an HR matter and are not covered here. The word
   "accommodation" means something different, with a different process and a
   different decision-maker. Say so.
2. **Program eligibility rules.** Housing Choice Voucher, HOME, LIHTC, and
   Section 8 program administration rules are not fair housing law, though they
   interact with it. If any Full Circle property is federally assisted, that
   needs its own course (VAWA, Section 504, and 24 CFR Part 5 obligations attach).

## One more structural suggestion

Add a short **Course C — Fair Housing for Association Boards** (~45 min,
non-employee audience) that you can deliver to the volunteer boards you serve.
Board members make the discriminatory statement or enforce the rule selectively
far more often than your managers do, and their conduct can create liability
that lands on the association you manage. This is a service differentiator as
much as a risk control.
