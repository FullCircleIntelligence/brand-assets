# Scenario banks

These are the practice and assessment scenarios the finished course must be able
to handle. Each is written so the correct answer depends on Vermont law, on the
role's actual authority, or on a procedural step — not on a vibe.

## Leasing

1. A caller asks whether the building is "family friendly" and how many kids live
   there. What do you answer, and what do you not answer?
2. An applicant has no Social Security number and offers a foreign passport and a
   Vermont driver's license. Your screening vendor's form requires an SSN.
3. An applicant's income is $2,400/month; contract rent is $1,500; the applicant
   has a voucher and will pay $450. Your criteria say "3x rent." Do they qualify?
4. A prospect mentions using a wheelchair and asks about ground-floor units. You
   have one on the ground floor and three upstairs. What may you say?
5. A prospect asks if you allow dogs. You have a no-pets policy. She mentions her
   anxiety. What is your next sentence?
6. An applicant discloses a felony conviction from 2011. Your criteria say "no
   felonies within 10 years." A colleague says "just deny it, it's policy."
7. A tester calls twice in one day using two different names and accents. What
   would the transcript show?
8. A current resident's adult son with an intellectual disability will be moving
   in as a live-in aide. Application, screening, and occupancy implications?

## Residential property manager

1. A resident responds to a lease violation notice for an unauthorized dog by
   saying the dog helps with her PTSD. Do you proceed with the notice?
2. A resident requests that his rent due date move from the 1st to the 5th because
   his SSDI deposits on the 3rd. Accounting says the system can't do it.
3. Two residents complain that a neighbor is harassing them with racial slurs.
   What is your obligation, and what is the risk of doing nothing?
4. An owner instructs you to stop accepting vouchers at their building.
5. A resident with a hearing disability asks for a visual doorbell and a visual
   smoke alarm. Which is an accommodation, which a modification, and who pays?
6. Six weeks after a resident filed an HRC complaint, her lease comes up for
   renewal and the owner wants a non-renewal.
7. A resident with a mobility disability asks for the parking space nearest the
   door, which is currently unassigned and used first-come-first-served.
8. A survivor of domestic violence asks for a lock change and to have her abuser
   removed from the lease.

## Association / HOA manager

1. The ARC receives an application to build a ramp to a front door in a limited
   common element. The covenants prohibit exterior alterations.
2. The pool rules say "no children under 16 without an adult" and "children's
   swim hours 10am-noon."
3. A board member posts in the community Facebook group that "the new family
   downstairs has too many kids running around."
4. An owner's assistance animal is a 90-pound dog. The covenants cap pets at 25
   pounds and prohibit "aggressive breeds."
5. A homeowner requests an assigned accessible parking space in a common element
   where all spaces are unassigned.
6. The board wants to see the medical documentation supporting an accommodation
   request before voting on it.
7. A rule against exterior religious displays is enforced against a mezuzah but
   not against Christmas wreaths.
8. The annual meeting is held in a second-floor room with no elevator. An owner
   who uses a wheelchair asks to attend.
9. The board wants to adopt a rental cap and requires board approval of all
   resales.

## Maintenance

1. While replacing a vanity, you notice the grab bars are in the way. What do you
   do?
2. A resident says while you're in her unit, "I really need a bar in the shower,
   I keep slipping." How do you respond, and what happens next?
3. You plow the lot and pile snow in the two accessible parking spaces because
   they're closest to the corner.
4. A resident's assistance animal is loose in the unit when you arrive for a
   scheduled repair.
5. Your ticket queue shows Building A repairs closed in 2 days and Building B in
   11 days. Building B is where most of the voucher households live.
6. A resident asks you not to tell anyone about her condition, which she mentioned
   while explaining the repair.
7. A co-worker makes a joke about a resident's gender identity in the van.
8. A pre-1978 unit turn: what are your EMP and lead-safe work practice obligations,
   and why does familial status make this a fair housing issue too?

## Accounting

1. A resident with an approved assistance animal has been charged $35/month pet
   rent for eight months.
2. A voucher holder's ledger shows a balance because the HAP payment posted late.
   Collections are auto-triggered at 10 days.
3. A resident emails you a letter from her therapist supporting an accommodation
   request. What do you do with it?
4. A manager asks you to note "ESA — dog" in the account memo field so everyone
   knows.
5. A resident requests a payment plan citing a disability-related income
   interruption. Your policy allows plans at manager discretion.
6. Your income verification worksheet excludes SSI and child support.
7. Late fees have been waived for four residents this year, all in one building,
   all by the same manager.
8. An eviction referral crosses your desk for a resident with a pending
   accommodation request.

## Executive / owner-level

1. An owner client says "I don't want to deal with Section 8."
2. Your insurer says covering a specific dog breed will raise premiums.
3. A HUD or Vermont HRC notice of complaint arrives naming the company and a named
   employee.
4. A property built in 1994 turns out to have no accessible route to the entrance.
5. Your screening vendor releases an AI scoring model. What do you need to know
   before enabling it?
6. Vermont's protected class list changes again. What is your process for
   re-issuing the courses and re-certifying staff?
