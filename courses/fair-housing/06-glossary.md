# Glossary

Shared by Course A and Course B. Terms marked **[VT]** are Vermont-specific or
have a Vermont-specific meaning. Terms marked **[HOA]** matter mainly in
association work. Terms marked **[$]** matter mainly to accounting.

---

## Core concepts

**Fair housing** — The body of federal, state, and local law prohibiting
discrimination in housing and housing-related transactions.

**Protected class (protected characteristic)** — A personal characteristic that
the law forbids using as a basis for a housing decision.

**Housing provider** — Anyone who sells, rents, manages, finances, or sets rules
for housing. Includes landlords, management companies, individual employees, real
estate agents, and **homeowners and condominium associations**. [HOA]

**Dwelling** — Any building or portion of a building occupied or intended as a
residence, and vacant land offered for construction of one.

**Covered transaction** — Rental, sale, terms and conditions, provision of
services or facilities, financing, brokerage, advertising, and post-move-in
treatment.

**Disparate treatment** — Intentionally treating someone differently because of a
protected characteristic. The classic form of discrimination.

**Disparate impact (discriminatory effect)** — A facially neutral policy that
disproportionately harms a protected group without a substantial, legitimate,
nondiscriminatory justification achievable by a less discriminatory means. See
24 C.F.R. § 100.500 and the Watch List.

**Perpetuation of segregation** — A discriminatory-effect theory based on a
policy's tendency to maintain segregated housing patterns.

**Facially neutral policy** — A rule that mentions no protected class but may
still be unlawful in its effect or application.

**Proxy** — A neutral criterion that functions as a substitute for a protected
characteristic (e.g. using ZIP code as a stand-in for race).

**Pretext** — A stated reason for a decision that is not the real reason.

**Steering** — Guiding a person toward or away from particular units, buildings,
floors, or neighborhoods based on a protected characteristic, including by
omission.

**Blockbusting** — Inducing sales or rentals by representing that people of a
particular protected class are moving into an area.

**Redlining** — Denying or pricing services differently based on the racial or
ethnic composition of an area.

**Selective enforcement** — Applying a rule to some residents or owners and not
others. The most common association-side claim. [HOA]

**Differential service** — Providing slower, worse, or fewer services to some
residents on a protected basis.

**Testing / paired testing** — Trained testers with matched profiles inquiring
about the same housing to detect differential treatment. Lawful and used in
Vermont. [VT]

**Tester** — A person who poses as a prospective renter or buyer to gather
evidence of differential treatment.

**Aggrieved person** — Anyone injured by a discriminatory housing practice,
including someone who is not the direct target.

**Complainant / Respondent** — The person filing a fair housing complaint / the
person or entity it is filed against.

**Conciliation** — Negotiated resolution of a complaint during the agency process.

**Vicarious liability** — An employer's liability for its agents' and employees'
discriminatory conduct. See 24 C.F.R. § 100.7.

**Direct liability** — Liability for one's own conduct, including failing to take
prompt corrective action against known harassment one had power to correct.

**Retaliation** — Coercing, intimidating, threatening, or interfering with a
person who exercised a fair housing right or assisted someone else in doing so.
42 U.S.C. § 3617; 9 V.S.A. § 4465. [VT]

**Interference** — Conduct short of outright denial that obstructs the exercise of
fair housing rights.

**Quid pro quo harassment** — Conditioning a housing benefit on submission to a
sexual or other unwelcome demand.

**Hostile environment harassment** — Unwelcome conduct severe or pervasive enough
to interfere with the use and enjoyment of a dwelling.

**Resident-on-resident harassment** — Harassment by one resident of another on a
protected basis, for which the provider may be liable if it knew and had the power
to correct it and failed to act.

## Protected classes

**Race / Color** — Also independently protected by 42 U.S.C. § 1982, which has no
exemptions.

**National origin** — Country of birth or ancestry, accent, name, ethnicity, and
language.

**Religion (religious creed)** — Belief, observance, practice, and religious
display.

**Sex** — Includes sexual harassment; courts have read it to include sexual
orientation and gender identity.

**Sexual orientation** — Expressly protected under Vermont law. [VT]

**Gender identity** — Expressly protected under Vermont law. [VT]

**Familial status** — Households with one or more persons under 18, pregnant
persons, and persons in the process of securing legal custody. Vermont phrases
this as intending to occupy a dwelling with one or more minor children. [VT]

**Age** — A Vermont protected class in housing with no direct federal analogue.
Interacts with HOPA. [VT]

**Marital status** — A Vermont protected class. [VT]

**Disability (handicap)** — A physical or mental impairment that substantially
limits one or more major life activities; a record of such an impairment; or being
regarded as having one.

**Receipt of public assistance** — A Vermont protected class functioning as a
source-of-income protection. Covers Housing Choice Vouchers, SSI, SSDI, Reach Up,
General Assistance, fuel assistance, and similar benefits. [VT] [$]

**Victim / survivor of abuse, sexual assault, or stalking** — A Vermont protected
class in housing. [VT]

**Citizenship and immigration status** — Added by Act 69, effective July 1, 2025.
[VT]

**Limited English Proficiency (LEP)** — Not itself a protected class; treated as a
national origin issue.

## Vermont-specific

**VFHPA** — Vermont Fair Housing and Public Accommodations Act, 9 V.S.A. § 4500
et seq. [VT]

**VHRC** — Vermont Human Rights Commission. Enforces the VFHPA and federal fair
housing law under contract with HUD. One-year filing deadline. [VT]

**Act 69 (2025)** — Housing Access for Immigrant Families. Added citizenship and
immigration status; restricts SSN requirements on rental applications. Effective
July 1, 2025. [VT]

**CVOEO Fair Housing Project** — Champlain Valley Office of Economic Opportunity's
fair housing program: education, outreach, enforcement, and paired testing in
Vermont. [VT]

**Essential Maintenance Practices (EMPs)** — Annual lead-safety obligations for
owners of pre-1978 Vermont rental housing under 18 V.S.A. § 1759, including window
well inserts, annual visual inspection, lead-safe repair of deteriorated paint,
common-area cleaning, and an annual compliance certificate. [VT]

**Vermont Rental Housing Health Code** — Statewide minimum habitability standards;
relevant to occupancy limits. [VT]

**VCIOA** — Vermont's Uniform Common Interest Ownership Act, 27A V.S.A., governing
common interest communities created on or after January 1, 1999. [VT] [HOA]

**Vermont Access Rules** — State accessibility requirements for public buildings,
alongside the Fire and Building Safety Code. [VT]

**FHAP** — Fair Housing Assistance Program. HUD's funding and referral
relationship with substantially equivalent state agencies such as the VHRC.

## Disability, accommodations, and modifications

**Reasonable accommodation** — A change, exception, or adjustment to a rule,
policy, practice, or service that is necessary to give a person with a disability
an equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling. Provider generally bears the
cost. 9 V.S.A. § 4503(a)(10); 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(B).

**Reasonable modification** — A physical change to the premises necessary for full
enjoyment. Requester generally bears the cost in private housing; the provider may
require interior restoration and **may not demand an additional security deposit**.
9 V.S.A. § 4503(a)(9); 42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(A).

**Interactive process** — The prompt, good-faith, two-way dialogue between
provider and requester to understand the need and identify a workable
accommodation. Not optional, and not satisfied by a form.

**Nexus (identifiable relationship)** — The required connection between the
person's disability and the requested accommodation.

**Undue financial and administrative burden** — A defense to a request, assessed
against the provider's overall resources and requiring a documented, quantified
analysis.

**Fundamental alteration** — A change that would fundamentally alter the nature of
the provider's operations or program. A defense.

**Direct threat** — A significant risk to the health or safety of others, or of
substantial physical damage to property, that cannot be eliminated or reduced by
another accommodation. Must rest on current, objective, individualized evidence —
never on stereotype or fear.

**Verification** — Confirmation from a reliable third party that the person has a
disability and that there is a disability-related need for what was requested.
Permitted only when the disability or need is **not known or readily apparent**.

**Reliable third party** — A source in a position to know about the disability and
the need: a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, psychologist,
social worker, therapist, case manager, peer support specialist, or comparable
source.

**Known or readily apparent** — Where the disability or need is already obvious or
already documented, verification may not be requested.

**Assistance animal** — An animal that works, provides assistance, or performs
tasks for a person with a disability, or provides emotional support that
ameliorates or mitigates identified symptoms or effects of a disability. In
Vermont, protected in housing **whether or not the animal has received special
training**. Not a pet; no pet fee, pet rent, or extra deposit. [VT]

**Service animal (ADA)** — Under the ADA, a dog (or in limited cases a miniature
horse) individually trained to do work or perform tasks for a person with a
disability. This narrower definition applies in public accommodations, not to
housing accommodation requests. Two permitted questions: is the animal required
because of a disability, and what work or task has it been trained to perform.

**Emotional support animal (ESA)** — Common shorthand for an assistance animal
that provides disability-related emotional support without task training.

**Live-in aide / attendant** — A person residing with an individual with a
disability to provide necessary support. Permitting one is a classic
accommodation; Vermont expressly protects reliance on attendants.

**Auxiliary aids and effective communication** — Interpreters, accessible formats,
assistive listening, and similar measures needed for equal access to information,
including at association meetings. [HOA]

**Accessible route** — A continuous unobstructed path connecting accessible
elements. Must be kept clear of snow, bins, and stored materials.

**Design and construction requirements** — The seven accessibility features
required in covered multifamily dwellings first occupied after March 13, 1991.
42 U.S.C. § 3604(f)(3)(C).

**Covered multifamily dwellings** — All units in buildings of four or more units
with an elevator; all ground-floor units in buildings of four or more units
without one.

**UFAS / ANSI A117.1 / ADA Standards** — Technical accessibility standards
referenced by different statutes.

**Section 504** — Rehabilitation Act provision applying to recipients of federal
financial assistance; shifts modification cost to the provider and sets accessible
unit percentages. 24 C.F.R. Part 8.

**Program accessibility** — The Section 504 obligation that a program, viewed in
its entirety, be accessible.

**Transfer as an accommodation** — Moving a resident to a different unit to meet a
disability-related need.

**Delay is denial** — The principle that an unreasonably slow response to a request
is treated as a refusal.

## Marketing, screening, and operations

**Ordinary reader standard** — Whether an ordinary reader would understand an
advertisement to indicate a preference, limitation, or discrimination.

**Equal Housing Opportunity logo / statement** — The required fair housing notice
in advertising and at the office.

**Audience targeting / lookalike audience** — Digital ad tools that can exclude
protected groups and create § 3604(c) exposure.

**Screening criteria** — The written, published, objective standards applied to
every applicant in the same way.

**Individualized assessment** — Evaluating an applicant's specific circumstances
rather than applying a blanket exclusion, especially for criminal history.

**Blanket ban** — A categorical exclusion (e.g. any criminal record ever) applied
without individualized assessment.

**Adverse action** — A denial or less favorable term based on a consumer report,
triggering FCRA notice and dispute rights.

**Tenant screening vendor / algorithmic scoring** — Third-party services that
score applicants. The housing provider owns the outcome. See the SafeRent
settlement.

**Occupancy standard** — A limit on the number of occupants per unit. Two persons
per bedroom is a starting point from the 1998 Keating Memorandum, not a safe
harbor; overly restrictive limits are familial status discrimination.

**Keating Memorandum** — HUD's 1998 occupancy standards memo, 63 FR 70982.

**HOPA / housing for older persons** — The 55+ and 62+ exemptions from familial
status liability, with verification and occupancy requirements. 42 U.S.C.
§ 3607(b). [HOA]

**80% rule** — The HOPA requirement that at least 80% of occupied units have at
least one occupant 55 or older. [HOA]

**Rent-to-income multiplier** — A screening ratio. Applying it to full contract
rent rather than the resident's portion is the most common way voucher holders are
screened out. [$] [VT]

**Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) / HAP payment** — Rental subsidy and the
housing assistance payment made to the owner. In Vermont, refusing vouchers
implicates the receipt-of-public-assistance protection. [$] [VT]

**Source of income discrimination** — Refusing or disadvantaging applicants
because of the lawful source of their income; reached in Vermont through the
receipt-of-public-assistance class. [VT] [$]

**Representative payee / third-party payer** — Someone who receives or remits
benefits or rent on a resident's behalf; accepting one can be an accommodation.
[$]

**Enforcement log** — The dated record of rule violations noticed and actions
taken, used to demonstrate consistent enforcement. [HOA]

**VAWA (housing provisions)** — Protections for survivors in federally assisted
housing, including emergency transfer and lease bifurcation. 34 U.S.C. § 12491.

**Lease bifurcation** — Terminating the tenancy of a household member who
committed abuse without terminating the survivor's tenancy.

## Association-specific [HOA]

**Common interest community** — Real estate in which owners are obligated to pay
for other real estate, such as common elements.

**Covenants / CC&Rs / Declaration** — The recorded governing documents. **Fair
housing law overrides inconsistent covenants; a covenant is never a defense.**

**Bylaws and rules** — Association governance and operating rules, subject to fair
housing review for both content and enforcement.

**Common element / limited common element** — Property owned or used in common /
reserved for one or more but fewer than all units.

**Architectural review committee (ARC)** — The body reviewing exterior and
structural changes. An accessibility-related application is a reasonable
modification request and must be routed to the fair housing process, not decided
on covenant grounds alone.

**Board of directors** — Elected governing body. Its statements, minutes, emails,
and social media posts are evidence.

**Assessment / special assessment** — Owner charges. Collections must be
consistent. [$]

**Rental cap / leasing restriction** — A limit on the number or share of leased
units; must be uniformly applied and examined for exclusionary effect.

**Resale or transfer approval** — Association approval rights over sales;
historically a vehicle for exclusion and a high-risk practice.

## Process and enforcement

**VHRC complaint** — A written, sworn complaint filed with the Vermont Human
Rights Commission within one year of the last discriminatory act. [VT]

**HUD complaint** — Filed with HUD FHEO, typically within one year; often referred
to the VHRC under FHAP.

**Dual filing** — The same complaint proceeding with both HUD and a state agency.

**Charge of discrimination** — The formal accusation issued after a finding of
reasonable cause.

**Consent decree / conciliation agreement** — Negotiated resolution, often with
monitoring, reporting, and mandatory training.

**Compensatory / punitive damages** — Damages for actual harm / to punish and
deter. Both are available in fair housing cases, along with attorney's fees.

**Injunctive relief** — A court order requiring or prohibiting conduct.

**Personal liability** — Individual employees can be, and are, named and held
liable personally.

**Loper Bright** — The 2024 Supreme Court decision ending judicial deference to
agency interpretations; relevant to how much weight HUD guidance and its
withdrawal carry.

**Withdrawn guidance** — An agency document no longer relied on by the agency. It
does not change the statute, the regulations, or state law.
